## The fixed token-sale schedule as an anti-insider property
*(captured 2026-07-27 — for Ch 4 "Staged issuance, the floor, and no governance" and Ch 15 "Isn't this just…?")*

A revnet is cool in itself partly because **the token sale's schedule is set in advance and immutable.** That fixedness is a feature with a specific benefit and a specific cost:

- **Benefit — it limits inside information.** When the issuance schedule (price steps, timing, decay) is fixed and public from launch, there is no privileged knowledge about *when* or *at what price* tokens will be released. No insider can time the sale, front-run a stage change, or trade on non-public schedule decisions, because there are no non-public schedule decisions — the schedule is the same for everyone and known in advance. It structurally limits insider manipulation of the sale itself.
- **Cost — it's rigid.** Management gives up the flexibility to adjust the raise to changing conditions (pause, reprice, accelerate, extend). A fixed schedule can't respond to a market shift or a mistake.

**Our position:** we recognize the trade-off and judge it worth it — **the reduction in insider manipulation outweighs the loss of management flexibility.** This is the same "rigid-on-purpose" logic as no-governance (Ch 4): you give up adaptability of the *terms* to buy credibility that the *terms* can't be gamed. Frame it as one more instance of the book's recurring thesis — *flexible about the work, rigid about the promise* — applied specifically to the sale schedule.
